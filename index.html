From Hiroshima,
making AI decisions verifiable.

The Hiroshima AI Assurance Council is an independent, industry-led initiative currently being established in Hiroshima, Japan. It develops HAAP as a proposed practical framework for AI-assisted decisions: verify the required conditions before adoption, then preserve the evidence chain after.

Hiroshima AI Assurance Protocol

Assured before action.
Verifiable afterward.

HAAP is a proposed assurance and interoperability layer connecting Business Profiles with agent, tool, and transport protocols. It does not replace HTTP, gRPC, Agent2Agent (A2A), or Model Context Protocol (MCP). It defines how requirements, accountability-relevant information, evidence, verification results, adoption states, and transition authorization records are linked and exchanged.

Pre-Execution Assurance
Before state change

Required conditions and evidence are evaluated before an AI-assisted decision is adopted as an organizational decision.

Claim–Evidence Separation
Claims are not verification

Inspection claims are evaluated against evidence independent of the claim, under trust requirements defined by the applicable profile.

Verifiable Record
Inspectable afterward

Requirements, accountability-relevant information, evidence references, verification results, adoption states, and transition authorization records are integrity-bound and preserved for later review, independent verification, and replay.

Implementation Evidence

From a tested logistics PoC
toward a common assurance protocol.

The first public reference PoC, completed as Phase 1, tested fixed ruleset binding, evidence inspection, independent recalculation, disposition generation, chain-stop control, digest binding, and replay verification for a pharmaceutical cold-chain handoff scenario.

Phase 1 implementation
The public repository documents the implemented functions, test scenarios, replay procedure, scope, and known limitations.

Relationship to HAAP
The PoC provides functional precursors that inform HAAP. It does not implement the complete HAAP record schema, domain-independent state separation, or multi-system interoperability.
